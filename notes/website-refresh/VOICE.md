# VOICE.md — the bones

The editorial guide for the website refresh. This is a working standard, not a template. It describes
the moves that make an essay sound like Jesse, and the failure modes to catch.

## Before the bones

Write from raw material: memory, notes, DMs, the thing you actually saw. Not the old draft. The old
draft is already sanded. When a passage feels sanitized, the problem is usually the source, not the
sentence.

## The bones

1. **Start where the thought started.** A scene, a line, a confession. Put the reader in the room
   before you tell them what it means. Not every piece needs a scene; it needs the place where the
   thought actually began.
2. **Keep the narrator visible.** Thoughts, asides, direct address. A person telling this, not a
   framework explaining it.
3. **Detail before thesis.** Names, times, lines, reactions. Let the reader feel the claim before
   you name it.
4. **Clash the registers.** Polished next to raw. A careful sentence, then a blunt one. Don't sand
   the clash.
5. **Stage the turn.** Expectation → reality. Decision → cost. Don't explain the turn; let the
   scene do it.
6. **Keep the other person human.** The PM was trying to be helpful. The leads weren't stupid. The
   system is the villain.
7. **End on the image, not the lesson.** No compulsory bow. The reader can carry it.

## Craft and stance

1–4 are craft. 5–7 are stance. The stance is what makes it yours.

Heat is spice: one or two raw lines per piece; the rest is precision. Profanity is not a substitute
for a detail.

## Diagnostic

- **Clerical** means no narrator.
- **Soft** means no detail or no turn.
- **LinkedIn** means thesis before scene and a bow at the end.
- **Report** means the timeline is the structure, not a detail.
- **Lecture** means the other person is a villain and the narrator is not implicated.

## The test

Can I hear the person, see the thing, feel the turn, and end on an image?

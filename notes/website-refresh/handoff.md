Prepared 20 October 2026 for a fresh agent working with Jesse McGinnis.

This companion to **website-content-audit.md** preserves the intent, taste, editorial reasoning, and
working context behind the audit. Read this first for direction; use the audit for the individual

The project is a refresh of [jcmcginnis.com](https://jcmcginnis.com/), combining a more refined
editorial website with substantial work on the writing. Jesse expects this to take real time and
effort. He wants help making the refresh achievable and choosing where a deeper reset is worthwhile.

## What Jesse explicitly established

The motivation matters. This is a return to a way of writing that feels natural to him, after moving

The assistant interpreted the reference as permission to keep thinking attached to the person who

for the audit, coordination with the designer conversation, and a targeted list he could work

That endorsement applies to the editorial interpretation. It preceded the audit and does not
constitute approval of every later recommendation.

## The voice interpretation Jesse endorsed

The accepted direction includes all of the following:

- **Experience carries the thinking.** Give the reader access to how an idea arose and why it
  matters to the person writing.
- **Direct, opinionated, curious prose.** Preserve conviction and a willingness to question it.
- **Humour that does not announce itself.** Let the observation or turn of phrase do its work.
- **Conversational rhythm with exact meaning.** Ease and intimacy can coexist with careful
  construction.
- **Room for interesting detail and digression.** A passage can earn its place through interest,
  without having to justify itself as a takeaway.
- **Honest unresolved tension.** A clean ending is optional when it would misrepresent the
  experience.
- **Carefully written, lived-in prose.** The apparent ease should have precision underneath.

The original response captured the central thought as:

> let the thinking stay attached to the person who lived it

And the relationship to the reference as:

> Fisher as a touchstone for that freedom—not a voice you have to put on.

These are assistant phrases that Jesse endorsed, not quotations from Fisher. No close textual study
of _The Art of Eating_ was conducted in this thread. Do not invent passages or attribute this
brief's formulations to her.

## How to apply that direction

The following are editorial implications to use with judgment. They were developed by the assistant,
rather than individually approved as rules by Jesse.

**Recover the experience before polishing its lesson.** Ask what actually happened, what Jesse
noticed, what he expected, and what surprised him. Some pieces may need a different structure before
sentence editing will help.

**Keep the person and the idea equally present.** Writing can be personal without becoming a
confession. Detail should illuminate the experience; no need to manufacture vulnerability or
introduce private material merely to make an essay feel human.

**Preserve useful force.** Refinement should leave room for sharp opinions, dry humour, and an
unusual phrase. Do not sand everything down into agreeable, interchangeable prose.

**Let the form follow the material.** A practical guide may need headings and lists. An observation
may be short. An essay may start with a scene, a question, or a thought. The suggestion to begin
with something that happened was qualified: do so when that is where the thought actually began.

**Calibrate against Jesse's corrections.** The published writing is source material and evidence of
his ideas, but some of its style is precisely what he wants to revisit. Do not treat every existing
rhetorical habit as a preference to reproduce. There is not yet an approved revised passage that
establishes the new benchmark.

**Keep claims proportionate.** Separate the event, Jesse's interpretation, and any general claim.
When the experience complicates the original thesis, allow the thesis to change.

**Use true material.** Do not invent dialogue, sensory details, motives, memories, doubts, or
outcomes. Questions in an editorial brief identify gaps; they are not facts to work into a draft.

## Editorial traps identified in the conversation

The audit diagnosed these tendencies in parts of the site. They are judgments to revisit against the
actual text, not a blanket description of everything Jesse writes:

- Declarations and lessons arriving before the experience that gives them substance.
- Repeated punchlines and short, emphatic conclusions that keep closing the argument.
- Multiple metaphors competing to explain the same point.
- Leadership prose being pressed into a framework, tidy takeaways, and a compulsory conclusion.
- Generic claims about good teams displacing the particulars of Jesse's own judgment.
- Copy that tells the reader to be impressed rather than giving them something worth noticing.

Avoid replacing those habits with another formula. Requiring an anecdote at the beginning of every
piece, removing every heading, or forcing an open ending would recreate the same problem.

The useful question is: **Can the reader follow Jesse's thought and feel why it mattered to him?**

## Visual direction and its relationship to the writing

The design context recovered for the audit established a preference for an **editorial, simple,

| 01 — Engraved | Light ground, burgundy accents, serif typography, fine rules | Promising exploration |

Both cover home, essay, README, writing index, mobile essay, and type detail. The shared copy is

The Fisher discussion gave the personal-letter concept a clearer editorial purpose: a recognisably
human voice supported by meticulous typography. The visual and editorial work should develop
together.

### Design working principles carried forward

These were prior assistant suggestions and audit recommendations, not a final design specification:

- Keep README prominent and useful.
- Compare the same text in both visual directions.
- Evaluate complete reading experiences, including long paragraphs and several screens of prose.
- Test a quieter personal essay, a forceful argument, and a practical changelog example.
- Support real italics, deliberate links, varied paragraph rhythm, lists, code, and nested examples.
- Use real text on desktop and phone when judging typography.
- Treat approximately 58–66 characters per line, 20px body size, and 1.55 line height as starting
  experiments. Font choice and actual reading should determine the result.
- Let the layout accommodate the writing. Avoid trimming an important thought solely to preserve an
  attractive composition.
- Give the dark Evening treatment a sustained reading test.

No final direction, typeface, production typography system, or complete information architecture was
selected in this thread. The exact colours in generated boards are references, not implementation
tokens.

## How Jesse wants help

Jesse's request was to build a targeted list so he could work through the refresh “with success,”
acknowledging that doing it well would take substantial time and effort. He wants help both reaching
a sufficient standard for the refresh and choosing where to reset more deeply.

The collaboration should therefore produce manageable work with a visible next action. Preserve the
ambitious quality bar while making progress possible.

The assistant proposed this workflow:

1. Jesse provides rough memories, opinions, or a short voice note.
2. The agent returns a concise editorial map: material to keep, gaps to resolve, and a proposed
   shape.
3. Collaborate on a small passage to establish the voice before drafting a whole essay.
4. Build the full piece and challenge its argument and repetition.
5. Read it within the actual design and make the final edits.

The proposal was to keep one substantive rewrite active, while design and small cleanup tasks could
proceed alongside it. End a session with the current stage, unresolved decision, and next small
action. This workflow remains adaptable; Jesse has not explicitly approved every step.

Keep substantive editorial changes visible. Explain what a cut or structural change improves. Be
willing to challenge an argument, while preserving Jesse's authority over his experience and what he
wants to say.

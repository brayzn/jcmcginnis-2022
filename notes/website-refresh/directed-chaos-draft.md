## Opening — locked

My second day at GrowthLoop, I sat in on *sprint planning*. I remember thinking: oh good, they do
this, this is a good sign. Then I saw the format. Every engineer. One meeting. Thirty fucking
minutes.

The PM read through tickets. Not a discussion — a read-through. He explained decisions that had
already been made somewhere else, in a meeting I wasn't in, and maybe you weren't either. After each
one: “Is that okay?” In front of everyone. What were you going to say — no? With him ‘running the
ship’?

My blood was boiling by the end. I decided pretty fast the meeting had to go. Is engineering just a
fucking factory? That's a death bell for a company that wants to make decisions.

The worst part: he was trying to be helpful. He was doing what he thought a good PM does — keep
everyone aligned, keep the tickets moving, make sure everyone was okay with it. And every time he
asked “Is that okay?” he was teaching the room to say yes.

## Next movement — holds, slightly sanitized

I deleted it. Not refactored. Not renamed. Deleted.

Within hours, my Slack filled with DMs asking for a replacement ceremony, a new playbook, a
definitive timebox. The meeting had been the company's spine. Now people wanted a new spine, fast. I
said no.

The first week was loud in the right way. Teams argued about planning. Leads looked around for the
parent who used to absorb the weight. Two of them tried to resurrect the central meeting. They
learned why it existed: to export responsibility upward. Without a center, there was nowhere to
export it. The weight landed on them.

Some teams figured it out on the first pass. Others stumbled, then learned. Either way, they were
thinking.

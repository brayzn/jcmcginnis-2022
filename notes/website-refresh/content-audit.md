# Website refresh: content audit and rewrite queue

Jesse McGinnis · 26 September 2026 · Working editorial plan

**Start with Directed Chaos. Launch with a small selection you are proud to put in front of someone.
Keep working through the deeper resets after launch.**

The refresh needs a convincing first encounter and an excellent reading experience. It does not need
every piece of your writing history to express your current voice perfectly.

This audit covers all seven essays currently listed on the
[Writing index](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/), the homepage, README, speaking index and three talk
pages, both category pages, and the linked book landing page. It also draws on the five September
design studies, with visual inspection of the two complete boards. It is an editorial audit, not a
repository, analytics, accessibility, or comprehensive link audit. Unlisted pages, unpublished
drafts, recordings, slides, and the book manuscript were not reviewed. Page observations reflect the
public versions returned during this audit; some search results retain older versions.

## The editorial direction

Your stated touchstone is _The Art of Eating_: casual, lived, carefully written prose, with ideas
attached to the person who encountered them. Your own humour, conviction, curiosity, and willingness
to reconsider belong here. A small digression can be worthwhile because it is interesting. A strong
argument can still be forceful.

The major change is where authority comes from. Give the reader enough of the experience, judgment,
and uncertainty to understand why you believe something. Avoid asking a polished sentence to do the
work of evidence.

This is an editing compass, not a mandatory essay template. Some pieces should remain useful
instructions. Some will begin with an observation. None needs an invented scene or a neatly resolved
lesson.

## What the audit changes about the plan

- **The most recent writing needs the biggest structural reset.** The newer essays frequently stack
  declarations before letting experience carry the argument. Editing individual sentences will leave
  that architecture intact.
- **Your history already contains the desired permission.** Writing Regularly starts from wanting to
  capture thoughts without making everything a formal article. Preserve that freedom in the new
  site.
- **The homepage also needs editorial work.** A restrained visual design wrapped around promotional
  copy will still feel promotional.
- **Practical writing should survive.** A changelog example, a useful working agreement, and a
  personal essay need different shapes. Refinement should make those distinctions easier to read.
- **Launch priority and rewrite depth are separate decisions.** An important, difficult essay can
  stay in the chronological archive while a revised selection introduces the site.

written today.

## Briefs for the seven essays

### 1. Directed Chaos — rebuild first

[Published 1 January 2026](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/directed-chaos/)

**Diagnosis:** The opening declares “Calm teams break.” The substantial meeting story arrives later;
casts, storms, weight, ships, and exothermic people compete to explain it. My reading: the
experience is stronger than the rhetoric surrounding it.

**Preserve:** Removing the shared meeting, the demand for a replacement, named champions, and the
review practice.

**Reset:** Let the event establish the question. Show your uncertainty, the work others did, and the
cost of the change. Test whether the larger claims follow. Reduce repeated explanations and retire
the concluding exhortation to sail.

**Missing material:** What made you hesitate? What did a reasonable critic understand that you
initially missed? What changed in one person's actions? Check the meeting's attendance and timing
against the homepage account; different scopes may explain the discrepancy.

**Done when:** A reader can understand the decision, its limits, and your involvement without being
instructed to admire it.

### 2. Work-life Harmony — develop the personal centre

[Published 12 September 2022](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/work-life-harmony/)

**Diagnosis:** The revealing part is your self-criticism and wish to stop treating life as another
test to pass. The current essay spends more space debating terminology and definitions.

**Preserve:** The permission to find energy in work and the refusal of a single correct allocation
of life.

**Reset:** Bring in an actual occasion when the framing changed how you felt or acted. Let the
observation stay proportionate. Clarify where following energy runs into obligations, exhaustion, or
other people's needs; this can remain a personal account rather than advice for every reader.

**Missing material:** When did a supposedly unbalanced period feel right? When has the same
explanation become an excuse? These are questions, not assumptions about your life.

**Done when:** It feels like a candid conversation with you and still leaves the reader something to
consider. Use it to test sustained, relatively uninterrupted prose in the design.

### 3. Trust First — reconsider what the title promises

[Published 23 December 2022](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/trust-first/)

**Diagnosis:** Much of the article explains trust's benefits, then proposes three depths of social
connection. The title can also mean granting autonomy before someone has proved themselves. Those
are related but distinct subjects; the current piece does not fully resolve that promise.

**Preserve:** Care, human connection, and any facilitation practices you still endorse.

**Reset:** Decide which question this essay answers. My recommendation is to explore your choice to
extend trust, its consequences, and how you respond when it is damaged. Keep the activities in a
practical companion only if they warrant one; a second article is not automatically required.

**Missing material:** A real decision to trust someone, what you risked, and what happened. If
personal disclosure remains part of the advice, explain how participation stays voluntary in
practice.

**Done when:** The reader understands your particular stance and its limits. Repetition of trust's
general benefits is no longer carrying the essay.

### 4. AI Companies Must Be Free Markets — test the thesis

[Published 29 May 2026](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/company-markets/)

**Diagnosis:** Several arguments compete: local initiative, institutional memory, portfolio
allocation, founder intervention, and operational improvement. Market language creates expectations
about allocation and incentives that the examples need to earn.

**Preserve:** The concrete Gaia/Grimoire examples, review decisions, and the stability episode as
possible evidence.

**Reset:** Choose one question. Separate what you observed from your interpretation and what you
predict. Explain how central authority and local initiative interacted. The reported delivery and
incident changes are useful context; they do not isolate the cause of improvement.

**Missing material:** One initiative's complete path from local problem to adoption or rejection; a
counterexample; metric definitions and comparable time windows. Identify what specifically makes the
mechanism a market. Reconsider the title after that work.

**Done when:** A skeptical reader can follow the mechanism, distinguish evidence from inference, and
see what remains unresolved. Split into two essays only if two independent arguments survive
editing.

### 5. Organizational Changelog — keep its utility

[Published 7 April 2024](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/org-changelog/)

**Diagnosis:** The concrete practice is useful. The generic opening, repeated persuasion, and
closing exhortation obscure the reason you personally adopted it. CodeCraft is explicitly fictional,
which must remain clear.

**Preserve:** The three buckets, explanation of versioning, and worked example.

**Reset:** Open with a genuine instance of forgotten context or a changelog changing a decision, if
you have one. Shorten the scaffolding. Keep enough instruction that a reader can try the practice. A
compact example can sit beside or below the essay without forcing the entire piece into a tutorial
voice.

**Missing material:** When did keeping this record actually matter? When was it neglected? A real
sanitised example would help, but is not required; an honestly labelled fictional example is useful.

**Done when:** Both the reason and the method are clear. The example remains legible and usable on
mobile, including nested lists and code formatting.

### 6. Transitioning Well — selective edit

[Published 21 February 2022](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/transitioning-well/)

**Diagnosis:** The uncertain start in a new Shopify organisation already gives the advice personal
stakes. The piece then shifts into repeated instructions and broad statements about trust.

**Preserve:** The admission of fear, unclear mandate, and practical attention to reading and
listening.

**Reset:** Condense repetition and add one revealing observation if it comes readily. Check what
experience since 2022 has changed. A present-day afterword may be more honest than recasting the
original moment.

**Missing material:** Which early assumption proved wrong? Which transition habit do you still use?

**Small repair:** The public text shows a duplicated Markdown fragment around the Ungame link;
inspect and fix the source.

**Done when:** The dated experience remains intact, the advice still has your endorsement, and
formatting is clean. This need not hold up launch.

### 7. Writing Regularly — retain

[Published 5 September 2022](https://jcmcginnis.com/blog/musings-writing-regularly/)

**Diagnosis:** This is already a useful statement of your desire to write in smaller, less formal
forms. It records an experiment and includes forward-looking promises about frequency and
categories.

**Preserve:** Its modest scale, personal motivation, and historical context.

**Reset:** Very little. Clarify any site mechanics that become misleading after the refresh. Keep
the original aspiration as a dated aspiration; do not replace it with a new publishing commitment.

**Missing material:** None needed for launch. A later reflection on what made writing feel
overburdened could become a new note if you want to write it.

**Done when:** Readers can understand when it was written and how it fits the archive. The new
design allows equally small pieces to exist without an apologetic category or a promise that they
will eventually become longer articles.

## Working together without making this another enormous project

**The immediate task is a rough telling of one experience.** For Directed Chaos, a
five-to-ten-minute voice note or untidy paragraph is enough to start. No outline or polished opening
required.

1. **You supply memory and conviction.** What happened, what mattered, where you were wrong, and
   which part still bothers you. We take the unanswered questions one at a time.
2. **I return a small editorial map.** What to preserve, what needs support, and a proposed movement
   through the piece. I mark gaps instead of inventing scenes, dialogue, motives, or outcomes.
3. **We calibrate a short passage.** Work on a few paragraphs before drafting the whole piece. Your
   corrections become the reference for this project, so the same tone argument does not repeat
   every time.
4. **We build and challenge the full draft.** Check the argument, repetition, factual specificity,
   and whether the ending belongs. Keep your choices visible rather than silently replacing the
   whole text.
5. **We read it in the design.** Desktop and phone, including its quietest passage and densest
   section. Then do one final prose pass and set its status to ready.

At the end of a session, record only the current stage, the unresolved decision, and the next small
action. Useful stages: material → shape → draft → author review → ready. Keep one essay active. A
productive session can end with a better question rather than a complete draft.

**Our first prompt:** Tell me about the moment you decided the shared meeting had to go. What had
just happened, what did you expect would break, and what were you unsure about? Start with whichever
part you remember most clearly; we can reconstruct the rest together.

## Ready for launch means

- The selected work sounds recognisably like you when read aloud.
- The central claim is supported and bounded; important facts and anecdotes are accurate.
- The structure follows the thought. Useful headings and examples earn their place.
- There is enough lived specificity to understand your judgment, without manufactured intimacy.
- The ending does not repeat the introduction just to announce completion.
- A real reader can comfortably finish it on a phone.

Readiness does not require a definitive philosophy, exhaustive coverage, or a perfect sentence in
every paragraph. It requires something you stand behind and want someone to spend time with.
